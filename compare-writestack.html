Donna vs WriteStack

WriteStack is the deepest Substack-only growth suite. Donna is a 2nd brain that closes the weekly decision in Claude. They overlap on scheduling. They are not the same product.

What WriteStack actually is

WriteStack is a Substack-only suite built around Notes. You queue Notes, sync them through a Chromium extension into Substack’s own scheduler, and get analytics, viral-notes research, AI drafting trained on your content, and (on higher tiers) a Claude MCP. Pricing as of 2026 runs about $23.99 / $32.99 / $99.99 a month, or lower on annual billing, with a 7-day trial and no free plan. One account covers one Substack. Notes only — not articles.

Its real strength is the layer Substack itself does not give you: which Notes drove subscribers, link-click history, benchmarks against other publications, a searchable feed of high-performing Notes, and a queue you can reorder. After Substack shipped native Notes scheduling in 2026, WriteStack kept growing because the queue and the data were the product, not the act of scheduling one Note.

What Donna actually is

Donna is a Claude MCP that loads a Creator Portrait from your Notion — voice, audience, strategy, past decisions, what’s already live — then proposes what to write, what’s working, and what’s next. You approve. A Chrome extension schedules the approved pieces. Nothing publishes in the sitting. Your portrait stays in your Notion. $30/month after a 7-day trial.

Who should pick WriteStack

You already know what to write. The pain is the queue, the analytics, and finding Notes that are working in your niche. You want a Substack-only dashboard and do not need a second brain that remembers last week’s strategy. WriteStack is the better tool for that job, and it is often cheaper.

Who should pick Donna

You already make money on Substack. The thing eating the week is deciding what to write, reading what’s working, and choosing what’s next — then briefing Claude again because the last chat forgot you. Donna loads that context from Notion and closes the week in one sitting. She is not a replacement for WriteStack’s analytics dashboard.
